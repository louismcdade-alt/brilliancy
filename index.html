Find your !! brilliant moves — Brilliancy for chess.com and Lichess

Brilliancy finds the brilliant moves in your chess games. Type any chess.com or Lichess username and a chess engine runs in your browser over your recent games, looking for the sound sacrifices — the moves chess.com's Game Review marks !!. There is no login, no account and no backend: your games are analysed on your own machine and nothing leaves the page.

What is a brilliant move in chess?

A brilliant move is a sacrifice that works. You give up material — a knight, a rook, sometimes a queen — and the position is still winning, because the material buys something worth more than itself: a mating attack, a decisive tempo, a king dragged into the open. It is not simply the best move in the position. chess.com ranks Brilliant above Best and Excellent precisely because a strong move that costs nothing is not brilliant; giving something up is what makes it one.

The other half of the label is that it should be hard to see. A sacrifice whose point only emerges several moves deep is brilliant in a way that an obvious recapture is not.

How Brilliancy finds them

Every move you played is checked for whether it puts material on offer, using a static exchange evaluation — so a piece that merely looks loose does not count, and neither does a capture that wins material outright. Moves that pass are handed to Stockfish 16, compiled to WebAssembly and running in a background thread in your browser, which is asked three things: is the position still good after the sacrifice, how much better is it than the best quiet alternative, and how much better does the move look at depth than it does at a glance. A small learned model weighs those answers together.

Is this the same as chess.com's !! ?

No, and it is worth being plain about it. chess.com's Brilliant label comes from their own Game Review and is not published in their public API, so this is a reconstruction of it, not the same number. It agrees with them often enough to be useful and disagrees often enough that you should look at the position yourself — every result shows the board, the move, and the reasons it was flagged, so you can judge each one on its merits.
